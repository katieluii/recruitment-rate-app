Where site-level prediction actually sits

Answering the question directly: in v2 the site and country layer is not part of the model. It is a lookup table built downstream from aggregated trial-level rates. The model never predicts a site. v3 changes that.

The honest limitation underneath both diagrams. Neither ClinicalTrials.gov nor AACT publishes per-site enrolment counts or per-site activation dates. No public source tells you that Site 47 enrolled 12 patients between March and August. Every site-level number in v2 — and most of what v3 can do — is inferred from trial totals, not observed.

v2 — what ships today

Two independent trial-level models. The site layer hangs off the side: it consumes the same training data but does not feed back into either prediction, and the country rates are trial-level rates attributed to every country the trial ran in.

learned model lookup / aggregation, not learned the gap
What this means in practice. Ask v2 for a Phase 3 oncology trial with 40 US sites and 20 Polish sites and it gives you one trial-level rate from the model, then separately tells you Poland's historical median is higher than the US. It never combines them: the duration prediction is identical whether you put the sites in Poland or Japan. The site mix changes the simulator's output but not the model's.

v3 — hierarchical enrolment process

The literature standard for this problem is a Poisson-Gamma process: each site enrols as a Poisson process with its own rate, and rates across sites are drawn from a Gamma distribution. That gives per-site prediction, principled uncertainty, and — critically — makes site mix an input to the duration, not a separate report.

Proposed v3 scope

V3.1 Right-censored duration — fixes a known bias

  • Pull ongoing trials (recruiting / active-not-recruiting), not just completed ones.
  • Refit duration as a survival problem: Weibull AFT and random survival forest first, DeepSurv only if it earns it. Score with C-index alongside our existing MAE.
  • This is the single highest-confidence improvement — we found the bias empirically and the literature names it independently.

V3.2 Poisson-Gamma site layer — makes site mix matter

  • Fit Gamma(α, β) over site rates per phase × therapeutic area × country, with shrinkage where sites are few.
  • Sequential stages: country initiation → site activation → per-site enrolment.
  • Monte Carlo forward simulation for the enrolment curve and its bands.
  • Calibrate against trials where we know total enrolment and site count — we can validate the aggregate even without per-site truth.

V3.3 Separate enrolment from follow-up

  • Duration = enrolment window + follow-up. Follow-up is largely readable from the parsed endpoint time frame, which we already extract but only use as one feature.
  • Removes the approximation that currently understates oncology recruitment rates.

V3.4 Text features over eligibility criteria

  • We use criteria counts. TrialEnroll shows the criteria text carries real signal.
  • Sentence embeddings, not a fine-tuned LLM — cheaper, deterministic, and testable in our existing harness.
  • Add sponsor identity, which the 2025 survival paper ranks as its #2 driver and which we already collect.
Sequencing. V3.1 and V3.3 are corrections to known defects and should go first — they are cheap and we can already measure them. V3.2 is the real capability jump and the biggest build. V3.4 is the smallest expected gain per unit of work; do it last, and only if the harness says the ceiling has not already been reached.
