Why the two columns disagree. The raw rate is enrolment divided by sites, so it falls almost exactly in step with site count — regressing it on site count gives a slope near −1. Sponsors also add sites precisely because sites are slow, so the two are chosen together. An unadjusted league table therefore partly ranks which countries happen to appear in small trials: China leads the raw Phase 3 table but sits below average once trial size is controlled. Rank the adjusted column; read the raw one as context.

Association, not attribution. A site inherits the rate of every trial it took part in, so one that appears in fast trials scores well — which may say more about the trials it is chosen for than about the site.

How the rate is calculated

rate = enrolment ÷ ( sites × recruiting months ) recruiting months = total duration − follow-up previously: total duration, follow-up included

Follow-up is read from the primary outcome's stated time frame and, where that will not parse, imputed from the median for that endpoint type. Correcting the denominator raises the Phase 3 median from 0.455 to 0.737 patients per site per month — the earlier figure understated recruitment by roughly 40% because it was dividing by the time spent watching patients as well as the time spent enrolling them.

  • No per-site enrolment is published anywhere. Neither ClinicalTrials.gov nor AACT records how many patients a given site enrolled, or when it opened. Everything here is inferred from trial totals.
  • A multi-country trial contributes the same rate to every country it ran in, because per-country splits are not published either. That makes this a comparator across countries rather than a measurement of any one of them — a country appearing mostly in large global trials inherits those trials' rates.
  • Thin cells are shrunk toward their parent. A country with six trials is pulled toward the indication's overall rate so it cannot top the ranking on noise. The trial count is shown on every row; treat anything under about twenty as directional.
  • Completed trials only. Trials still running are absent, and they are disproportionately the slow ones — measured at a 2018 vantage point, Phase 3 duration looked 20.9 months when it was truly 24.6. Rates here are therefore optimistic, and more so for recent years.
